Outcome Switch Monitor

Did a clinical trial report the primary outcome it registered? Human-validated rules, applied at scale by AI.

    Check a trial outside the corpus

    Paste any NCT number. The server fetches the current record from ClinicalTrials.gov, recovers the version registered before the trial started from the Internet Archive, and asks the AI model to classify the change. Works for trials with posted results whose registry history was archived (crawl of February 2025). Takes 20–60 s.

    What is measured

    For each trial we compare the primary outcomes registered before the trial started (the last registry version submitted no later than the start month, recovered from ClinicalTrials.gov history archived by the Internet Archive) with the primary outcomes posted with the results. Secondary outcome titles are used only to detect a primary outcome that was demoted.

    Rules

    Written rules derived from Zarin et al. (2011) classify each trial as identical, rewording, clarification (the registry was vague and the results add detail without contradicting it), substantive change, or undeterminable. Changes are typed (removed, demoted to secondary, added, replaced, time frame, metric, measure/definition) and graded major (removed, replaced, demoted, or a new efficacy outcome) or minor. When in doubt, clarification is preferred over change.

    Human ground truth

    • 95 trials drawn from the 4,969 non-identical trials (stratified, survey-weighted estimates).
    • Each trial annotated twice, independently, by two of three annotators; agreement before arbitration: 73 % on “change or not” (Cohen's kappa 0.44).
    • Rules clarified by the team, then every disagreement settled by an arbiter outside the annotation team. No label or arbitration was produced by AI.

    AI at scale

    • Four models released before April 2026 were compared on the 95 human-labelled trials; Gemini 2.5 Flash was kept (kappa 0.73 on “change or not”, close to the human count of changes).
    • It then classified the other 4,874 non-identical trials for about US$3.0.
    • Measured bias on the human sample: it finds slightly more substantive changes (34 % vs 32 %) and fewer major ones (11 % vs 17 %). AI figures are exploratory; the human estimate is the reference.

    Limits

    • A discrepancy is not misconduct: a change can be justified and reported in the publication (CONSORT).
    • Registry vs posted results only; journal articles are not covered.
    • Validation rests on 95 trials, which were also used to choose the model and refine the rules, so AI agreement is probably optimistic.
    • Registry history depends on the Internet Archive copy of February 2025.
